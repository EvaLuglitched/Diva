How you play

Two ways to drive

On a Mac or a Windows PC you stand in front of a webcam and your whole body drives. On Apple Vision Pro you sit in the cockpit and the headset reads your hands and head. Same elephant, same Candy Town, same race.

macOS · Windows

Webcam: stand up and move

One player does every job, or three players split them left to right. The keyboard works at any time, with or without a camera.

One lap

  1. Pick a mech skin (raise one arm for the next one), choose 1 or 3 players, then START.
  2. Camera check: stand back until your head to waist is in the picture. When every card turns green, hold still for 5 s and the game saves your standing pose.
  3. Race: shoot targets and candy, grab golden targets, go ON FIRE and finish the four tasks.
  4. The ice wall: only a Mega Bubble smashes it, so save your charge.
  5. Finish: a hero card, a highlight replay and the leaderboard.

One lap

  1. Look at START and pinch. The camera flies over Candy Town and drops you into the cockpit.
  2. Pick your paint: pinch to try the next skin, then CHOOSE.
  3. Learn the moves: glowing ghost hands show each one, then you practise until every move ticks.
  4. Set off: raise both hands to eye level.
  5. Race: the coach points to each task; the same targets, tasks and ice wall, then the hero moment and the leaderboard.

How we made it

Six days, three people, one elephant

Built on the course's DigiPhant starter: a Unity scene and a Python camera bridge. No custom model was trained. Pretrained MediaPipe models find the bodies and hands, and our own rules turn the movements into controls.

Oct 8Foundations

  • Set up the DigiPhant Unity project with the savannah course and the webcam, and got a first three-player gesture demo running.

  • Built Candy Town around the course: pastel streets, targets, a rocket, the HUD and a race intro under a starry sky.

  • Put a D.Va-style mech suit on the elephant, wired the gestures to it and built the match flow: four skins, the ultimate, a highlight replay and a leaderboard.

Oct 9–11Two ways to play

  • Started the first-person Vision Pro version on its own branch: a cockpit on the elephant's back, driven by hands and head.

  • Added keyboard play next to the gestures, so anyone can try the game without a camera.

  • Added trial export: every run writes its route and events for analysis.

Oct 12Three players, one camera

  • Rebuilt three-player detection so all three bodies are found every frame without stalls, tuned for Apple silicon.

  • Made the gestures feel right: thumbs-up refill, steering by tilting the shoulders or head, and a camera check page with role cards and a stick-figure demo of every move.

  • Filled the race: candy, ON FIRE, golden targets, the log task, the finish arch and the ice wall. Then we locked the gesture settings for one and three players.

Oct 13Polish and release

  • A console-style look at 120 fps on M1 and M2 Max MacBook Pros, and the app icon.

  • Version 1.1: the camera tracking is built into the Mac and Windows downloads. Tested with 93 automated gesture tests, an automatic gold lap, a three-player run, a live camera round and a clean Windows machine.

  • Version 1.2 (Mac): whoever stays in front of the camera keeps playing, the tracking no longer crashes with several people in view, the Mac always uses its own camera, and the tracking restarts itself if it ever stops.

  • Version 1.3 (Mac): walking is much easier for beginners, and the camera tracking is back to 30 fps.

Under the hood

From webcam to elephant

Webcam→MediaPipe pose + hands→Gesture rules→Local UDP→Unity elephant

Tracking

MediaPipe Pose Landmarker (full), Hand Landmarker and the pretrained Gesture Recognizer for thumbs-up, version 0.10.35. Everything runs on the player's computer and nothing is uploaded.

Gestures

Calibrated rules on the body: arm swings for walking, shoulder or head tilt for turning, hand pose for spraying. The settings are locked and covered by 93 automated tests.

Game

Unity 6 with the Universal Render Pipeline: depth of field in cutscenes, motion blur while boosting, and a frame-rate guard that holds 120 Hz on ProMotion screens.

First start

Install in a minute

The very first camera start on a computer takes a little longer while the tracking prepares itself once: about 17 s on a Mac and 6 s on Windows. After that it takes a few seconds.

macOS Download

  1. Unzip and drag Diva.app into Applications.
  2. Open it. The app is signed but not notarised, so macOS stops it the first time: click Done.
  3. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway and confirm. On macOS 14 or older, right-click the app and choose Open instead.
  4. Click Allow when Diva asks for the camera.

Windows Download

  1. Unzip the whole folder. Do not run the game from inside the zip.
  2. Run Diva.exe. If SmartScreen appears, click More info › Run anyway.
  3. The camera starts by itself. If the picture stays dark, open Settings › Privacy & security › Camera and allow desktop apps.

Vision Pro Download

  1. The build is development-signed and installs only on the team's registered headsets.
  2. With the headset paired, drag Diva VR.app into Xcode › Devices and Simulators › Installed Apps.
  3. Allow hand tracking the first time the app opens.
